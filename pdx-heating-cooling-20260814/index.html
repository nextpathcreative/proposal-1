Why this works

Two halves of the same job.

Getting more jobs off Google comes down to two questions: how often do you come up when someone searches, and what happens when you do? Both have to work — one without the other, and the job goes to someone else.

Half one — getting found

When someone nearby searches "AC repair near me" or "furnace repair in Portland," Google hands them a list of companies. The higher up that list you sit, the more people see you — and the more of them click through. And it's not all-or-nothing: every spot you move up puts you in front of more people than you're reaching now.

Where you land isn't luck. It comes down to how complete and how active your listing is, whether your details match everywhere else online, and how recent your reviews are. All of it, I can work on.

I'll be straight with you about the goal, though. I'm not going to promise you the top three — anyone who promises that is guessing. What I can do is move you up from where you sit now and keep at it, because those spots get contested constantly and nobody holds one by sitting still.

The part nobody mentions

Ranking isn't something you win once.

This is the thing I want to be straight with you about, because it's the difference between money well spent and money wasted. Anyone who offers you a one-time cleanup and walks away is selling you something that stops working. Four things are always moving underneath you:

The other companies aren't standing still

Every heating and cooling business near you wants the same top spots. The ones actively working on it climb. The ones who fixed things once and stopped quietly slide down — usually without noticing until the phone gets quiet.

Google keeps changing what it rewards

What put a listing on top two years ago isn't what does it today. Nobody sends out a memo when it changes. You find out by watching the results and reacting.

How people search keeps changing

The way people look for you doesn't hold still — AI especially is changing how they search. What puts you in front of them today won't be what does it next year.

Your reviews get old

Nearly three quarters of people weigh how recent your reviews are. Your 21 are an asset — but a five-star review from 2021 carries less than one from last month. It needs feeding.

So the job isn't "fix the listing." The job is keeping an ear to the ground — watching what shifted, what the companies around you changed, and how people are finding you — and adjusting before you lose ground. That's what I do, week to week, for as long as you want me on it.
